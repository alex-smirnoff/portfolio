UX Research

Project Background

Learning and development is a core HR function. The company runs subject-specific training, onboards new employees, and maintains a library of learning materials. Before this project, most of that work was managed manually. The process consumed learning managers' time, made materials difficult to access, and excluded some employees because of IT infrastructure constraints.

Constraints

Device access creates a second constraint.

Personas

The project context and constraints showed that the primary problems involved discovering information and engaging with learning content—not administering it.

Learning managers already enter training and onboarding data in the HRIS. In the proposed model, that data flows into the learning product automatically, while the product handles onboarding notifications, reminders, and progress tracking.

Learning managers therefore continue to administer programs through the HRIS. The new product focuses on employees participating in corporate learning.

Insights

  1. The research revealed three core journeys: mandatory onboarding for new hires, optional training for all employees, and browsing learning materials.
  2. Required onboarding should stand apart from optional programs and clearly show both progress and the completion deadline.
  3. The system should remind the user about deadlines.
  4. Employees who are not marked as new hires in the HRIS can take onboarding on request. The interface should identify the course as onboarding without showing a deadline reminder.
  5. Optional learning should use a scannable card library. Visual and text cues should show participation status and the current stage of the process at a glance.
  6. Every training page should use the same clear, easy-to-scan information structure.
  7. The interface should show the current approval status and the next step in the process.
  8. Notifications should cover the key stages of training: registration opening, approval, the start of class, schedule changes, and cancellation.

UX / Product Design

Product Goal

Create a single corporate learning platform that gives every employee convenient access to training, onboarding, and learning materials—whether or not they have a corporate mailbox.

The product should:

  • make learning opportunities easier to find and access;
  • make statuses and organizational stages clear;
  • deliver timely reminders about important events and deadlines.

Platform Strategy

The product had to work without a corporate computer or mailbox and deliver timely process notifications. A mobile-first app was therefore the most practical platform.

From Business Process to UX

The business had no predefined way to translate its existing processes into an app. I interviewed stakeholders to understand those processes before designing the experience.

I mapped the interview findings in BPMN.

Training Management

The end-to-end process involves three participants. The employee’s journey—and their interactions with a manager and learning manager—was central to the design because it determines the states and information shown in the product. The other two roles operate through the HRIS, so they matter primarily to integration and business logic.

The key user need was a clear, consistent view of available training. I brought every opportunity into one place and presented it through a unified collection of interactive cards.

I translated each step of the BPMN flow into an interface state and user interaction.

Onboarding Course

I also analyzed the original onboarding format: an introduction from the learning manager, video materials, and a group discussion.

To remove the dependency on group formation and live facilitation, I transformed it into a self-paced digital course.

BeforeAfter
Introduction A brief course introduction explains the structure and sets clear expectations.
Video session Content moves directly into course pages and is divided into sequential learning modules.
Discussion Short questions after each module check comprehension and reinforce the material, following a familiar online-learning pattern.
Course completion After completing every module, users receive a confirmation and completion message. If they miss the deadline, the system reminds them to finish the course.

The group session becomes an asynchronous onboarding course that new employees can take immediately, while the learning manager no longer has to run the same session for every cohort.

Notifications cover the full deadline cycle: the 14-business-day requirement, an approaching deadline, an overdue course, and successful completion.

Learning Materials

Accessing learning materials became a separate journey. Because some employees cannot use SharePoint or OneDrive, the content library lives directly in the product.

The primary experience is a card library where users can scan titles and file types quickly.

The “All Materials” screen supports title search and filtering by file type.

Structured search works best when users already know what they need. I therefore proposed a second discovery path: an AI assistant for finding relevant learning opportunities.

The assistant can access the learning library and training catalog. Instead of naming a specific document or course, users describe a work task or development need—for example, “I want to improve my client negotiation skills.”

Based on the request, the assistant recommends relevant materials and available training that can help address the need.

This creates two complementary discovery modes: a structured search for known materials and an intent-based AI search. In the second mode, users only need to describe the problem they want to solve.

UX Decisions

Main Page

Mobile screens are scanned from top to bottom, so the home page follows a clear priority order: required content first, high-priority optional content next, and the broader learning library below. The onboarding course leads, followed by corporate training and then learning materials.

Because onboarding is required, it is visually distinct from other training. A deadline indicator and progress bar help users estimate the remaining effort and finish on time.

Completed and Canceled Training

The company repeats some training topics, and employees may attend the same program more than once. Keeping every completed course in the home feed would gradually bury active content. Completed training therefore moves to the user's profile in the tab bar, where learning history and certificates can live together.

Completed trainingCanceled training

Canceled sessions remain in the main card library for five business days so affected users can see the update, then disappear.

Pre-Training Messages

Automation should not make the experience feel impersonal. I added optional comments to automated messages so managers can provide context when approving requests, canceling or rescheduling training, or sharing updates during a program.

With a commentWithout a comment

This preserves a human presence behind each decision. It is not a chat; the previous process relied on email rather than real-time messaging. Instead, each training has a message thread—limited during approval and fully available to participants—and the same updates appear in a global feed accessible from the home page. Comments are optional, but the channel is always available.

AI-Assisted Discovery

Alongside its written response, the AI assistant presents recommended training and materials as cards. The visual format separates actionable results from the explanation and makes them easier to scan.
